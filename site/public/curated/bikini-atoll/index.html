Pikinni. A home, a history, and an ocean that remembers.

A small ring in a very big ocean.

In the northern Marshall Islands, a necklace of coral islands surrounds Bikini’s vast lagoon. Coconut palms meet white coral sand; the reef gives way to water in a hundred shades of blue.

Its Marshallese name is Pikinni. This is the ancestral home of the Bikinian people, whose relationship with these islands reaches far beyond the view.

Beauty doesn’t erase the past.

In 1946, the United States displaced Bikini’s community to make way for nuclear testing. Operation Crossroads sank ships in the lagoon that July. Further testing left an enduring mark on the land and on generations of Marshallese people.

Inscribed in 2010, the UNESCO World Heritage site preserves evidence of the beginning of the nuclear age. The story of Bikini is also a continuing story of home, displacement, and the resilience of its people.

A fleet, held in time.

The aircraft carrier USS Saratoga, the Japanese battleship Nagato, and USS Arkansas rest in Bikini’s lagoon after the 1946 tests. Saratoga lies upright; Nagato and Arkansas are overturned. Marine life now inhabits their weathered structures.

This is an artistic exploration. The landscape, wreck details, distances, and depths are simplified; it is not a navigation or dive-planning chart.
